# **Obesity and anxiety symptoms: a systematic review and meta-analysis**

**Sohrab Amiri · Sepideh Behnezhad**

Received: 21 September 2018 / Accepted: 19 January 2019 / Published online: 18 February 2019 © Springer-Verlag GmbH Austria, ein Teil von Springer Nature 2019

#### **Summary**

*Background* Obesity and anxiety are the two most common health problems and increased body mass index can be lead to anxiety.

*Objective* The aim of this meta-analysis was to investigate the frequency of anxiety symptoms in people who are obese/overweight.

*Methods* For this purpose the authors systematically searched keywords in the databases PubMed, Scopus, PsycINFO and Google scholar through August 2018. After a comprehensive review, 25 studies were included into the meta-analysis.

*Results* Results of the meta-analysis showed that the frequency of anxiety in obesity had a pooled odds ratio (OR) of 1.30 and a 95% confidence interval (CI) of 1.20–1.41 and in overweight had an OR of 1.10 and CI of 1.00–1.21. Comparison between obesity/overweight and normal weight showed high frequency of anxiety in obesity/overweight with respect to subgroups (sex, obesity and anxiety assessment, adjusted/unadjusted, anxiety duration and age). Evaluation of 25 studies included in the metaanalysis showed publication bias.

#### **Adipositas und Angstsymptome: systematische Review und Metaanalyse**

#### **Zusammenfassung**

*Grundlagen* Adipositas und Angst sind die beiden häufigsten Gesundheitsprobleme, ein erhöhter Body-Mass-Index kann zu Angst führen.

*Zielsetzung* Ziel dieser Metaanalyse war es, die Häufigkeit von Angstsymptomen bei Menschen mit Adipositas bzw. Übergewicht zu untersuchen.

*Methodik* In den Datenbanken PubMed, Scopus, PsycINFO und Google Scholar wurde anhand von Schlüsselwörtern systematisch nach bis 08/2018 publizierten Arbeiten recherchiert. Nach umfassender Überprüfung wurden 25 Studien in die Metaanalyse aufgenommen.

*Ergebnisse* Die Ergebnisse der Metaanalyse zeigten für die Häufigkeit von Angst bei Adipositas eine gepoolte OR ("odds ratio") von 1,30 und ein 95%-Konfidenzintervall (KI) von 1,20-1,41, für die Häufigkeit von Angst bei Übergewicht eine gepoolte OR von 1,10 und ein 95%-KI von 1,00-1,21. Beim Vergleich zwischen normalgewichtigen und übergewichtigen/adipösen Menschen zeigte sich eine hohe Frequenz von Angststörungen bei Adipositas/Übergewicht in Bezug auf Untergruppen (Geschlecht, Assessment von Adipositas und Angst, angepasst/unangepasst, Angstdauer und Alter). Die Auswertung von 25 in die Metaanalyse einbezogenen Studien ergab ein Publikationsbias.

*Schlussfolgerung* Insgesamt deuten die Ergebnisse darauf hin, dass Angststörungen häufiger bei überge-

wichtigen bzw. adipösen Menschen auftreten als bei normalgewichtigen.

### **Introduction**

One of the most common psychiatric disorders is anxiety [\[24\]](#page-16-0). Anxiety and its symptoms are associated with impairment in daily life [\[48\]](#page-17-0) and use of health care systems [\[28\]](#page-16-1). Anxiety disorders include: generalized anxiety disorder, selective mutism, social anxiety disorder, panic disorder, specific phobia, agoraphobia, panic attack, other specified anxiety disorder, substance/medication-induced anxiety disorder, unspecified anxiety disorder and anxiety disorder due to other medical conditions [\[4,](#page-15-0) [5\]](#page-15-1). Anxiety symptoms include muscle tension, tenderness, restlessness and fatigue [\[4,](#page-15-0) [5\]](#page-15-1). Various factors such as socio-economic factors [\[26\]](#page-16-2), violence [\[47\]](#page-17-1) and traumatic events [\[22,](#page-16-3) [29\]](#page-16-4) have been associated with anxiety and its symptoms. Burden of different anxiety disorders is estimated to be between 0.4 and 1.1% [\[38\]](#page-16-5). One study reported a prevalence of 10.6% in the past year for anxiety and a prevalence of 16.6% for lifetime anxiety [\[46\]](#page-17-2). Extensive efforts have been made to identify trajectories of anxiety. Obesity and overweight are two of the most important somatic factors in this regard.

Obesity is another public health problem that is rising steadily [\[16,](#page-16-6) [27\]](#page-16-7). It is estimated that one third of the adult population worldwide is overweight measuring the body mass index (BMI) [\[33\]](#page-16-8). Over the past few decades, the prevalence of obesity has increased dramatically [\[39\]](#page-16-9) and prevalence of obesity in children and adolescences increased from 1975 to 2016 [\[1\]](#page-15-2). It is well known that obesity and overweight are associated with disabilities and mortality [\[10,](#page-15-3) [34\]](#page-16-10). High weight is the major risk factor of death and 70% of deaths are associated with cardiovascular disease caused by high BMI [\[3\]](#page-15-4).

Obesity is associated with various psychological functions and in this regard studies showed a positive association between obesity and psychiatric disorders [\[12,](#page-16-11) [25,](#page-16-12) [42\]](#page-16-13). Accordingly, some studies showed that obesity is associated with the increase of lifetime

Although extensive original studies have been conducted on obesity/overweight and anxiety, there are a few systematic review studies in this regard. Based on what was said, reviewing more studies and their sub-components can provide valuable insights about frequency of anxiety in obesity/ overweight. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to do a systematic review and a meta-analysis of the studies on the associations between obesity and anxiety. Another goal of this study was to comprehensively investigate gender, anxiety duration and age differences with regard to associations between obesity/overweight and anxiety.

# **Methods**

#### *Protocol and registration*

For this research, the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [\[37\]](#page-16-18) protocol was used as a standard practice.

#### Search strategy

In order to find the articles published on the association between obesity (obesity and overweight) and anxiety, a systematic review was done in PubMed, Scopus, PsycINFO and Google scholar until August 2018, using MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) keywords. Articles were limited to the ones in English language.

<span id="page-3-0"></span>**Fig. 3** Forest plot of obesity, overweight and anxiety frequency

# Selection criteria

The following criteria were used for including and excluding the studies into the meta-analysis: 1) having cross-sectional, prospective cohort and retrospective study designs; 2) defining obesity or overweight as the exposure; 3) defining anxiety as the outcome; 4) reporting odds ratio or other statistical indictors (such as hazard ratio and risk ratio or mean and standard deviation or proportion); 4) having a reference group (non-obese); 5) having the age of 10 or over; 6) excluding the studies conducted on pregnant women.

#### *Quality assessment of included studies*

The quality assessment of the included studies were done independently by the authors according to 5 criteria using Effective Public Health Practice Project Criteria [\[6\]](#page-15-5). The quality assessment of the items is presented in Table 1 in the ESM.

# Data extraction

All data were extracted using a standard form that included the following: author(s), publication year, country, continent, study design, population, age, sex, sample size, obesity type (obesity, overweight and severely obese), obesity assessment tools, anxiety, anxiety assessment tools, anxiety duration, odds ratio and adjusted variables. For all studies, an adjusted odds ratio with 95% confidence intervals was extracted. However, in some studies, the odds ratio was unadjusted; most of these studies were crosssectional. In some studies the odds ratio was not reported; in these studies the odds ratio was calculated using other indicators (e. g. mean and standard deviation or proportion). In order to extract the needed data for subgroup analysis, the odds ratio was extracted for any subgroup. The studies in which pooled odds ratio was not reported, first, the logarithm of the odds ratios and confidence intervals was calculated, then the pooled odds ratio was calculated.

<span id="page-4-0"></span>**Fig. 4** Forest plot of obesity, overweight and anxiety frequency in men (**a**) and women (**b**)

This procedure was also carried out for subgroup analysis. The reviewers independently extracted all the above information. In this study the meta-analysis was performed using a random-effects method.

#### Statistical analysis

According to WHO classification BMI was categorized into BMI <18.5 (underweight), 18.5–24.9 (normal weight), 25–29.9 (overweight) and ≥30 (obese) [\[2\]](#page-15-6). The studies in which obesity and overweight were defined based on BMI percentiles were included in the metaanalysis. Anxiety was defined according to Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders [\[4\]](#page-15-0) and International Classification of Diseases [\[13\]](#page-16-19) (ICD); therefore studies that defined anxiety based on clinical interviews and self-reports were included into the meta-analysis. Meta-analysis was performed using the Stata-14 software (Stata Corp., College Station, TX, USA). Subgroup analysis was also conducted on obesity (obesity and overweight), sex, obesity assessment tools (self-reported and measured), anxiety assessment tools (questioner-based and interview based), adjusted or unadjusted, anxiety duration (current anxiety, 12-month and lifetime anxiety prevalence), age (adult and adolescent) and study design (crosssectional and prospective cohort). In order to measure heterogeneity, Cochrane χ<sup>2</sup> (Qtest) and *I*<sup>2</sup> statistic were used [\[20,](#page-16-20) [23\]](#page-16-21). To identify the origin of heterogeneity, random effects and subgroup analysis were calculated. To determine publication bias different methods were used including funnel plots, Beeg and Egger test and the trim-and-fill method [\[7,](#page-15-7) [14,](#page-16-22) [15\]](#page-16-23).

All eligible studies which evaluated the frequency of anxiety symptoms in obesity and overweight were included in the study. Through reviewing, 25 studies were included in the meta-analysis. A complete description of the characteristics of these studies are given in Table 1 in the ESM.

#### Quality of included studies

Studies included in the meta-analysis were assessed based on five quality dimensions. The results of quality assessment showed, 5, 13 and 7 studies had low, moderate and high risks of selection bias respectively.

#### Publication bias and heterogeneity

The funnel plot of 25 studies which consist of 22 crosssectional studies and 3 prospective cohort studies. Results of the Begg test (*P*= 0.105) did not show any publication bias, but the results of the Egger test (*P*= 0.001) declared publication bias. Also, the trimand-fill method [\[14\]](#page-16-22) imputed 11 missing studies based on random effects. The heterogeneity of the 25 studies which were included in the meta-analysis based on BMI ≥25 was equal to I2= 94.1% which indicates a high degree of heterogeneity [\[21\]](#page-16-24). In order to identify the origins of heterogeneity, a subgroup analysis was done comprehensively. After subgroup analysis, heterogeneity was calculated for all subgroups. In some subgroups including sex, anxiety duration, age and study design's analysis, heterogeneity dramatically improved. Heterogeneity changes were no so large in some subgroups' analyses.

# **Discussion**

Present study was done in order to do a systematic review and a meta-analysis of anxiety frequency in obesity and overweight. For this purpose, 25 articles including 22 cross-sectional studies and 3 prospective cohort studies were considered for the meta-analysis based on eligible criteria. Findings revealed that individuals with obesity and overweight have more anxiety compared to non-obese persons. Evidence of the association between obesity/overweight and anxiety is sometimes contradictory, which in turn, it was one of the reasons for doing this meta-analysis. The relationship between overweight and obesity and anxiety can also affect other possible factors. Emotional eating is a factor which refers to eating as an emotional response to negative excitement [\[30,](#page-16-25) [31,](#page-16-26) [35\]](#page-16-27), and it has been shown that this factor is related to gaining weight [\[8,](#page-15-8) [9,](#page-15-9) [18\]](#page-16-28). Also, some other studies show BMI as a mediator between stress and emotional eating [\[36,](#page-16-29) [49\]](#page-17-4). On the other hand, there may be an inverse relationship, that is, anxiety disorder will lead to weight gain. Women are more likely to respond to negative emotions through eating. In fact, this is a response strategy in women which can lead to weight increase [\[32\]](#page-16-30).

Regarding gender differences in the frequency of anxiety in obesity and overweight, our findings showed that the association between obesity and overweight is somewhat stronger in women than men. Obesity and overweight contribute to anxi-

One factor that makes women with overweight and obesity experience more anxiety can be due to a stigma related to weight and the discrimination that is more severe about women [\[11\]](#page-15-10). On the other hand, overweight women are more likely to be dissatisfied with their weight and experience social problems as compared to women of desirable weight [\[19,](#page-16-31) [41\]](#page-16-32).

Findings about obesity/overweight and anxiety based on self-reported or measured BMI showed that the association is significant in measured and selfreported obesity. Various tools have been used to measure anxiety in studies included in the metaanalysis. Accordingly, the findings were classified into two main categories of interview-based and questionnaire-based; results declared no significant association between overweight and anxiety in interview-based and questionnaire-based anxiety, but this association was significant in obesity/interviewbased anxiety and questionnaire-based anxiety. The presented findings about frequency of anxiety in obesity/overweight were based on three anxiety duration including lifetime, 12-month and current anxiety. Obesity was associated with all three durations of anxiety, though these associations were somewhat different. Other subgroup analysis was age-based analysis. Accordingly, subgroup analysis for adults and adolescents were carried out and findings indicated that in both age groups, obesity was associated with anxiety frequency. There are no evidence of heterogeneity in adolescents.

Gariepy and et al. But the present study, which included both obesity and overweight, showed an association among obesity/ overweight and anxiety, though somewhat of a difference was found between obesity and overweight. Also, the findings of present meta-analysis showed a low level of heterogeneity in prospective cohort studies in comparison with cross-sectional studies. There were some limitation in included studies. It was unclear whether the anxiety that was evaluated as the outcome was anxiety symptoms or diagnostic anxiety disorder.
